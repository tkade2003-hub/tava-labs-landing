PDRN, Explained: The Ingredient Behind the Glow

PDRN went from clinic shelves in Seoul to the front of our formula. What it is, where it comes from, and what it does in a daily serum.

Every formula has a headline ingredient. Ours is PDRN — polydeoxyribonucleotide — and it earned the spot. Here is what it actually is, without the hype.

What PDRN is

PDRN is a DNA-derived ingredient, sourced from salmon, that has been used in Korean skincare and clinic settings for years before arriving in daily serums in the West. In cosmetic use it is valued for supporting skin that looks firm, even and luminous — the qualities that add up to what people call “glass skin.”

Why it anchors our formula

The Glow Brightening Serum is a brightening formula, and PDRN is paired with a supporting cast chosen to cover the full picture:

  • Acetyl hexapeptide-8 — a peptide associated with the look of smoother, firmer skin.
  • Sodium hyaluronate — the salt form of hyaluronic acid; draws water into the skin's surface for a plump, hydrated look.
  • Glycerin and sodium PCA — humectants that help skin hold moisture through the day.
  • Panthenol — provitamin B5; conditions and comforts, keeping the formula gentle enough for twice-daily use.

What PDRN is not

It is not a drug, not an injection, and not a promise. In a cosmetic serum, PDRN supports the appearance of healthy, radiant skin as part of a consistent routine. The results that matter are gradual and cumulative — which is exactly why the serum is built as a daily habit, twice a day, with a plan that ramps you in.

One headline ingredient, a cast that backs it up, and nothing in the bottle that does not have a job. That is the formula philosophy — and you can read every ingredient, in full, on our Ingredients page.
